The treatments

Two signals, one goal: get chronically painful tissue pain-free again.

Chronic tendon and fascia pain often isn't ongoing damage — it's tissue that has healed poorly, frequently resulting in scar tissue that is strong but still symptomatic. Both treatments are designed to give it a fresh stimulus to repair.

◉
Extracorporeal shockwave therapy · ESWT
Shockwave

Short, high-pressure acoustic pulses travel through the skin into the injured tendon, fascia, or bone. Cells respond to that mechanical stimulus with new blood-vessel growth, collagen remodeling, and a reset of local pain signaling — kick-starting repair in tissue that had plateaued. It's one of the most-studied non-surgical options for chronic tendon pain.

  • Feels likeA strong, rapid tapping over the sore spot. Intensity is set to your tolerance.
  • TargetsThe precise spot that hurts — we locate it by feel and feedback.
  • EvidenceDecades of research, including multiple randomized trials.
⬡
Extracorporeal magnetotransduction therapy
EMTT

High-energy pulsed electromagnetic fields — far stronger and faster-oscillating than conventional PEMF devices — reach deep into muscle, tendon, and bone without any contact pressure. The field is thought to stimulate cell metabolism and repair across a broader area than the shockwave head covers.

  • Feels likeLittle or nothing — at most a light twitch or tingle. It's painless.
  • TargetsA wider, deeper zone around the injury, including bone.
  • EvidenceNewer. Early randomized trials are promising, but the research base is much smaller.
+

Why pair them? Shockwave treats the focal point of the injury; EMTT treats the tissue around and beneath it. Combining the two in one visit is a common protocol — and combination trials are where EMTT has shown the most benefit so far, e.g. better outcomes than shockwave alone in a randomized trial for rotator cuff tendinopathy.

Is it right for you?

Safety first.

Shockwave and EMTT are low-risk for most healthy runners, but they aren't for everyone.

Good candidates

You'll likely get the most out of it if:

  • The pain has lasted roughly three months or more
  • It's a tendon, fascia, or known overuse injury — not a mystery
  • Rest, rehab exercises, or load changes alone haven't resolved it
  • You're willing to pair treatment with smart training changes
Don't book if any of these apply

Talk to us first — and in most cases your doctor:

  • Pregnancy
  • Pacemaker, defibrillator, or other implanted electronics
  • Metal implants in or near the treatment area
  • Bleeding disorder or blood-thinning medication
  • Cancer, or a tumor near the treatment area
  • Infection, open wound, or skin condition at the site
  • Steroid injection in the area in the last 6 weeks
  • Under 18 (open growth plates)
  • A new, undiagnosed injury — e.g. a suspected fracture or tear

FAQ

Good questions.

Does it hurt?

Shockwave is uncomfortable but brief — a strong, rapid tapping right over the sore spot. We start low and adjust to your tolerance. EMTT is painless; most people feel little or nothing.

How many sessions will I need?

Most protocols are 3–6 sessions, about a week apart. Some runners notice a change after the first couple; tendon remodeling continues for weeks after the last session, so the full effect often shows up later.

Can I keep running?

Usually, yes, as symptoms allow. We suggest keeping the day of treatment and the day after easy, and avoiding big jumps in load while you heal. Coaching athletes get this built into their plan.

Should I take anti-inflammatories or ice afterward?

We generally suggest skipping over-the-counter anti-inflammatories and ice for a couple of days after each session, since the treatment works partly through your body's own healing response. Don't stop anything your doctor has prescribed.

Is this a replacement for seeing a doctor or physical therapist?

No. We don't diagnose injuries. If your pain is new, severe, getting worse, or you're unsure what it is, get it evaluated first — shockwave and EMTT work best on a known problem.
